Edit and sign

Sign PDF files

Draw your signature with a finger, a pen or the mouse, then drag it into place on a page of your PDF and choose its size. Usugami adds it to the page as a picture.

    Works on phones and computers. Your files stay on your device.

How to sign a PDF

  1. Open the tool. Press the Open Sign PDF button at the top of this page. The tool opens in your browser, on a phone, tablet or computer, with nothing to install and nothing to sign up for.
  2. Choose your PDF. Press Choose PDF and pick the PDF to sign, or drop it there on a computer. If it’s protected with a password, Usugami asks for it first.
  3. Draw your signature. Press Draw your signature, sign above the line and pick black or blue Ink, then press Done. Clear lets you start again.
  4. Place it on the page. In a PDF with more than one page, pick the page under Page to sign (the last page is chosen first). Drag the signature into place, or tap where it should go, and set its Size.
  5. Sign and save. Check the File name and press Sign PDF, then Save to Downloads. Where your browser can share files, Share sends it to another app.

What you can do with Sign PDF

  • Sign on a white pad, above a line, in black or blue ink.
  • Your signature goes on the page as a sharp picture with a see-through background, trimmed to your ink.
  • Place it on a preview of the page: drag it, or tap or click where it should go. The move buttons nudge it a step at a time, and on a computer the arrow keys do too.
  • Size goes from half an inch on the signature’s longer side up to as big as the page.
  • A new signature starts in the lower right of the page.
  • Form fields and comments under the signature become part of the page, so it sits on top of them as in the preview. The other pages stay as they were.
  • The new PDF is named after yours with _signed added, and you can change it under File name.
  • To change your signature, tap it under Signature. Closing the pad without pressing Done keeps the one you had.

Your files stay on your device

Usugami works on your files right in your browser. Nothing is uploaded: the files you pick and the PDFs it makes never leave your device.

There are no ads, no tracking and no cookies, and you don’t need an account. Tools you have used once keep working offline while your browser keeps their files; a text-recognition language or font used for the first time needs the internet.

Questions

Is this a digital signature?

No. Your signature is added to the page as a picture of what you drew. It isn’t a certified digital signature.

Is my signature saved?

Only in the page’s memory while the web app is open, so you can sign another PDF without drawing it again, and reloading or closing the page forgets it. It’s never saved on its own, only in the PDFs you sign. Like every PDF you save, a signed PDF is also kept on Recent files in this browser until you remove it.

Can I sign a password-protected PDF?

Yes. Usugami asks for the password when you choose the PDF, and the signed PDF has no password. PDFs locked with a certificate (digital ID) can’t be opened.

Can I add the date or my name as typed text?

Sign PDF adds only the signature you draw. To add typed text such as a date, use Text in Edit PDF.

Is Usugami free?

Yes. Usugami is free and open source, with no ads, no account and nothing to buy.

Does Usugami put its name on my PDFs?

Not on its pages, unless you turn on the Usugami watermark switch on the result screen, which adds a small “Usugami” in the corner of each page. Once on, it stays on for the PDFs you make next until you turn it off. The PDF’s document properties do name Usugami as the program that made it.

Which browsers does it work in?

Current Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, on phones, tablets and computers. Some buttons, such as Share and Save as…, show only in browsers that support them. On an Android phone you can also install the Usugami app.
