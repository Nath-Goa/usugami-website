Convert Excel to PDF

How to convert Excel to PDF

  1. Open the tool. Press the Open Excel to PDF button at the top of this page. The tool opens in your browser, on a phone, tablet or computer, with nothing to install and nothing to sign up for.
  2. Choose your workbooks. Press Choose Excel files and pick one or more .xlsx workbooks. On a computer, you can also drop the files there.
  3. Set up the pages. Leave Page size and Orientation at As in the file to keep each sheet’s own setup, or choose one for every sheet. Turn Fit columns to page width and Gridlines on or off.
  4. Convert to PDF. Check the File name of each workbook, then press Convert to PDF. With several workbooks the button says how many, and each one becomes a PDF of its own.
  5. Save your PDF. On the result screen, press Open or Save to Downloads, or Share and Save as… where your browser offers them. Several PDFs can also go into one ZIP file with Share or save as ZIP.

What you can do with Excel to PDF

  • Each visible sheet starts a new page and prints as Excel prints it: print area, title rows and columns, page breaks, paper size, orientation, margins, scale and Fit to.
  • Numbers, currencies, percentages, dates and times show as their number formats show them, and formulas show the last values saved in the file.
  • Keeps font sizes, bold, italic, colors, fills, borders, merged cells, wrapped text, column widths, row heights and pictures, and prints headers and footers with page numbers, dates and sheet names.
  • Page size: As in the file, A4, US Letter, US Legal, A3 or A5. Orientation: As in the file, Portrait or Landscape.
  • Fit columns to page width is on at first: wide sheets shrink so their columns fit across the page, but never below half size. Turned off, columns that don’t fit go on more pages.
  • Gridlines adds light gray lines between cells, as on screen. Borders, and gridlines a sheet is set to print, always show.
  • Hidden sheets, rows and columns are left out, and so are charts, shapes, text boxes, comments and conditional formatting.

Your files stay on your device

Usugami works on your files right in your browser. Nothing is uploaded: the files you pick and the PDFs it makes never leave your device.

There are no ads, no tracking and no cookies, and you don’t need an account. Tools you have used once keep working offline while your browser keeps their files. A few parts download the first time they’re needed, such as a text-recognition language, a font or the HEIC photo decoder, and need the internet then.

Questions

Are formulas recalculated?

No. Cells with formulas show the last values saved in the workbook, and a formula saved without a value shows the formula itself.

How do I fit a wide sheet on the page?

Keep Fit columns to page width on, as it is at first, and choose Landscape for Orientation to give the columns more room. A sheet set to Fit to in Excel keeps that setting.

Are charts included in the PDF?

No. Charts are left out, and so are shapes and text boxes. Pictures placed on the sheet are kept.

Can I convert an old .xls file?

Not directly. Save it as .xlsx in Excel or another app first, then convert the .xlsx file.

Is Usugami free?

Yes. Usugami is free and open source, with no ads, no account and nothing to buy.

Does Usugami put its name on my PDFs?

Not on its pages, unless you turn on the Usugami watermark switch on the result screen, which adds a small “Usugami” in the corner of each page. Once on, it stays on for the PDFs you make next until you turn it off. The PDFs it makes do name Usugami in their document properties, as the program that made them.

Which browsers does it work in?

Current Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, on phones, tablets and computers. Some buttons, such as Share and Save as…, show only in browsers that support them. On an Android phone you can also install the Usugami app.
