Compress PDF files

Make PDF files smaller in your browser by reducing the images inside them. Text and drawings stay sharp, and you can compress one PDF or several at once, each with its own level and file name.

  • Open Compress PDF

    Works on phones and computers. Your files stay on your device.

How to compress a PDF

  1. Open Compress PDF. Press the Open Compress PDF button at the top of this page. The tool opens in your browser, on a phone, tablet or computer, with nothing to install and nothing to sign up for.
  2. Choose your PDFs. Press Choose PDFs and pick one PDF or several at once. On a computer you can also drop PDFs onto it, and Add PDFs adds more later.
  3. Pick a compression level. Under Compression, choose Less, Recommended or Extreme; Recommended is chosen at first. With several PDFs, Compression for all PDFs sets every one, and each PDF can have a level of its own.
  4. Check the file name. Each new PDF is named after its original with _compressed added, such as Report_compressed.pdf. Type another name in File name if you like.
  5. Compress. Press Compress PDF (with three PDFs it reads Compress 3 PDFs). Under each new PDF that got smaller, Usugami shows its original size and how many percent smaller it is.
  6. Save the result. Press Save to Downloads. With several PDFs you can also use Share or save as ZIP to get them all in one ZIP file.

What you can do with Compress PDF

  • Three levels, Less, Recommended and Extreme, with Recommended chosen at first.
  • Only images change. Text stays text, so it stays sharp, selectable and searchable, and links, form fields and bookmarks stay as they are.
  • Images are shrunk to suit the space they fill on the page and are never enlarged. A smaller copy replaces an image only when it saves space.
  • Black-and-white (1-bit) images, such as black-and-white scans of text, are left as they are.
  • Compress several PDFs at once: Compression for all PDFs sets the level for every PDF, and each PDF’s card has its own Compression and File name.
  • When Usugami hands back your original file unchanged, because compressing would save less than 1%, the result says “Already compact: the original was kept”.
  • Password-protected PDFs open once you type the password, and the compressed PDF has no password.

Your files stay on your device

Usugami works on your files in your browser, on your device, and nothing is uploaded: the files you pick and the PDFs it makes never leave your device.

There are no ads, no tracking and no cookies, and you don’t need an account. Tools you have used once keep working offline.

Questions

Does compressing a PDF make the text blurry?

No. Only images are made smaller. Text stays text, so it stays sharp and you can still select and search it.

What’s the difference between Less, Recommended and Extreme?

They set how much detail images keep. Less aims for 200 pixels per inch for the space an image fills on the page, Recommended for 150 and Extreme for 100, and each uses a lower JPEG quality than the one before. Images are never enlarged.

Why didn’t my PDF get smaller?

Compress PDF only reduces images, so a PDF that is mostly text, or whose images are black-and-white (1-bit) or very small, has little to shrink. When the saving would be under 1%, Usugami hands back the original file unchanged (“Already compact: the original was kept”), unless it has to save the PDF again, for example to remove its password; then it says “This PDF couldn’t be made any smaller.”

Can I compress a password-protected PDF?

Yes. Usugami asks for the password when you add the PDF, and the compressed PDF is saved without a password. PDFs locked with a certificate (digital ID) can’t be opened.

Is Usugami free?

Yes. Usugami is free and open source, with no ads, no account and nothing to buy.

Does Usugami put its name on my PDFs?

Only if you turn on the Usugami watermark switch on the result screen, which adds a small “Usugami” in the corner of each page. It’s off unless you turn it on.

Which browsers does it work in?

Current Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, on phones, tablets and computers. Some buttons, such as Share and Save as…, show only in browsers that support them. On an Android phone you can also install the Usugami app.
