Convert Word to PDF

Turn Word documents (.docx) into PDFs in your browser. Styles, tables, pictures, headers and footers carry over, and links stay clickable.

How to convert Word to PDF

  1. Open the tool. Press the Open Word to PDF button at the top of this page. The tool opens in your browser, on a phone, tablet or computer, with nothing to install and nothing to sign up for.
  2. Choose your Word files. Press Choose Word files and pick one or more .docx documents. On a computer, you can also drop the files there.
  3. Name your PDFs. Each document gets a File name box, filled in with its own name, which you can change. To add more documents, press Add Word files.
  4. Convert to PDF. Press Convert to PDF. With several documents the button says how many, such as Convert 3 files to PDF, and each one becomes a PDF of its own.
  5. Save your PDF. On the result screen, press Open or Save to Downloads, or Share and Save as… where your browser offers them. Several PDFs can also go into one ZIP file with Share or save as ZIP.

What you can do with Word to PDF

  • Keeps the page size and margins, portrait and landscape sections, headings and other Word styles, bold, italic, underlined and colored text, bulleted and numbered lists, and page breaks.
  • Tables keep their column widths, merged cells, borders and shading, and their header rows repeat on each page.
  • Headers and footers carry over with their page numbers, including different first-page and even-page ones.
  • Links stay clickable, both to web addresses and to places inside the document, such as the lines of a table of contents.
  • Text stays searchable and selectable. Only the letters the document uses are embedded, and pictures are stored at no more than 150 pixels per inch.
  • Convert several documents at once: each becomes its own PDF, with a name you can change before converting.
  • Charts, SmartArt and pictures in formats the browser can’t decode, such as EMF and WMF, show as light gray boxes.

Your files stay on your device

Usugami works on your files right in your browser. Nothing is uploaded: the files you pick and the PDFs it makes never leave your device.

There are no ads, no tracking and no cookies, and you don’t need an account. Tools you have used once keep working offline while your browser keeps their files; a text-recognition language or font used for the first time needs the internet.

Questions

Can I convert an old .doc file?

Not directly. Save it as .docx in Word or another app first, then convert the .docx file.

Can I convert a password-protected Word document?

No. Remove the password in Word, save the file, then try again.

Are footnotes and comments included?

No. Footnotes, endnotes, comments and deleted text are left out, and text set in several columns becomes a single column.

Why do some lines break differently than in Word?

Usugami’s own fonts stand in for the document’s, so some lines may break in different places than in Word.

Is Usugami free?

Yes. Usugami is free and open source, with no ads, no account and nothing to buy.

Does Usugami put its name on my PDFs?

Not on its pages, unless you turn on the Usugami watermark switch on the result screen, which adds a small “Usugami” in the corner of each page. Once on, it stays on for the PDFs you make next until you turn it off. The PDF’s document properties do name Usugami as the program that made it.

Which browsers does it work in?

Current Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, on phones, tablets and computers. Some buttons, such as Share and Save as…, show only in browsers that support them. On an Android phone you can also install the Usugami app.
