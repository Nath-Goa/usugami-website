Convert PowerPoint to PDF

Turn PowerPoint presentations (.pptx) into PDFs in your browser, one page per slide at the size of the slides. Hidden slides are left out.

How to convert PowerPoint to PDF

  1. Open the tool. Press the Open PowerPoint to PDF button at the top of this page. The tool opens in your browser, on a phone, tablet or computer, with nothing to install and nothing to sign up for.
  2. Choose your presentations. Press Choose PowerPoint files and pick one or more .pptx presentations. On a computer, you can also drop the files there.
  3. Name your PDFs. Each presentation gets a File name box, filled in with its own name, which you can change. To add more presentations, press Add PowerPoint files.
  4. Convert to PDF. Press Convert to PDF. With several presentations the button says how many, such as Convert 3 files to PDF, and each one becomes a PDF of its own.
  5. Save your PDF. On the result screen, press Open or Save to Downloads, or Share and Save as… where your browser offers them. Several PDFs can also go into one ZIP file with Share or save as ZIP.

What you can do with PowerPoint to PDF

  • One page per slide, in the presentation’s order and at the size of its slides. Slide numbers still count the hidden slides that are left out.
  • Keeps slide backgrounds, shapes with their fill and outline, pictures cropped as on the slide, and the shapes and pictures of the slide master and layout behind each slide.
  • Text keeps its alignment, spacing, bold, italic, underline, size and color, with bullets, numbering and slide numbers, and stays searchable and selectable.
  • Text too long for its box shrinks to fit, when PowerPoint would shrink it.
  • Tables keep the presentation’s table styles and merged cells. SmartArt is drawn from the copy PowerPoint saves with it, and ink, equations and embedded objects from the pictures PowerPoint saves of them.
  • Usugami’s own fonts stand in for the presentation’s, and charts show as gray boxes with their title.
  • Convert several presentations at once, each into its own PDF.

Your files stay on your device

Usugami works on your files right in your browser. Nothing is uploaded: the files you pick and the PDFs it makes never leave your device.

There are no ads, no tracking and no cookies, and you don’t need an account. Tools you have used once keep working offline while your browser keeps their files. A few parts download the first time they’re needed, such as a text-recognition language, a font or the HEIC photo decoder, and need the internet then.

Questions

Are speaker notes included in the PDF?

No. Each slide becomes one page, and speaker notes, comments and animations are left out. A video shows as its poster picture.

What happens to hidden slides?

They are left out of the PDF, so a presentation whose slides are all hidden has nothing to convert.

Can I convert an old .ppt file?

Not directly. Save it as .pptx in PowerPoint or another app first, then convert the .pptx file.

Are links clickable in the PDF?

No. Links keep their color and underline, but they aren’t clickable.

Is Usugami free?

Yes. Usugami is free and open source, with no ads, no account and nothing to buy.

Does Usugami put its name on my PDFs?

Not on its pages, unless you turn on the Usugami watermark switch on the result screen, which adds a small “Usugami” in the corner of each page. Once on, it stays on for the PDFs you make next until you turn it off. The PDFs it makes do name Usugami in their document properties, as the program that made them.

Which browsers does it work in?

Current Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, on phones, tablets and computers. Some buttons, such as Share and Save as…, show only in browsers that support them. On an Android phone you can also install the Usugami app.
