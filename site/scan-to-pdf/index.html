Scan documents to PDF

Take a photo of a paper page and Usugami looks for the page on it, lets you adjust its corners and straightens it. Choose a look such as Enhanced or Black & white, then turn your pages into a PDF.

How to scan a document to PDF

  1. Open the tool. Press the Scan a document button at the top of this page. The tool opens in your browser, on a phone, tablet or computer, with nothing to install and nothing to sign up for.
  2. Take a photo of the page. Scan document starts on the Images to PDF screen, where scans and images become a PDF. Press Take photo there: on a phone it opens your camera app, and on a computer it lets you pick an image instead.
  3. Adjust the corners. On Crop page, Usugami looks for the page. Drag the corners or sides onto its edges if needed, then press Next.
  4. Choose a look. On Filters, pick a look such as Enhanced or Black & white, and set Brightness and Contrast. Press Take another for the next page, or Done.
  5. Create the PDF. Press Convert to PDF, choose a Compression level and, if you like, turn on Make text searchable (OCR). Then press Create PDF.
  6. Save your PDF. Press Save to Downloads to keep your PDF.

What you can do with Scan document

  • Usugami looks for the page on each photo and, when it finds it, puts the corners on its edges. Drag any of the four corners or four sides to adjust them, with a magnifier while you drag.
  • The page inside the corners is straightened, so a page photographed at an angle comes out straight.
  • Reset puts the corners back where Usugami placed them, and Whole image keeps the entire photo.
  • Seven looks, each shown on your page: Original, Lighten, Enhanced, Super enhanced, Grayscale, Black & white and Ink saver.
  • Brightness and Contrast go from -100 to +100. With two or more pages, Apply to all pages gives every page the same look, brightness and contrast.
  • The first photo starts on Enhanced, and each photo after it starts with the look you chose last, so your pages match.
  • Make text searchable (OCR) adds the text of your pages to the PDF as invisible, searchable text, in English, French, Spanish or Hindi.
  • The location and other hidden metadata of your photos are left out of the PDF.

Your files stay on your device

Usugami works on your files right in your browser. Nothing is uploaded: the files you pick and the PDFs it makes never leave your device.

There are no ads, no tracking and no cookies, and you don’t need an account. Tools you have used once keep working offline while your browser keeps their files; a text-recognition language or font used for the first time needs the internet.

Questions

Can I scan a document on a computer?

Yes. On a computer, Take photo opens a file picker, so you can pick a photo of a page and crop it the same way. You can also add images with Photos or Files, or drop or paste them.

How do I scan several pages into one PDF?

On Filters, press Take another after each page and Done after the last one. In Create PDF, keep Merge all images into one PDF on to get one PDF with a page for each photo.

Can I scan photos I already took?

Yes. Add them with Photos or Files, then press the crop button on a page to open Crop page and Filters for it. On the web app’s Home, Upload images in the Scan document card takes each image you pick through both steps, one after another.

Is Usugami free?

Yes. Usugami is free and open source, with no ads, no account and nothing to buy.

Does Usugami put its name on my PDFs?

Not on its pages, unless you turn on the Usugami watermark switch on the result screen, which adds a small “Usugami” in the corner of each page. Once on, it stays on for the PDFs you make next until you turn it off. The PDF’s document properties do name Usugami as the program that made it.

Which browsers does it work in?

Current Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, on phones, tablets and computers. Some buttons, such as Share and Save as…, show only in browsers that support them. On an Android phone you can also install the Usugami app.
