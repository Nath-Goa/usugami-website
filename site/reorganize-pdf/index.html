Organize

Rearrange PDF pages

Reorganize PDF shows your PDF’s pages in a grid. Drag them into a new order, turn or remove them, and add a blank page or the pages of another PDF before you save.

How to rearrange PDF pages

  1. Open the tool. Press the Open Reorganize PDF button at the top of this page. The tool opens in your browser, on a phone, tablet or computer, with nothing to install and nothing to sign up for.
  2. Choose your PDF. Press Choose PDF and pick the PDF (on a computer you can also drop it on that card). If it has a password, Usugami asks for it.
  3. Move, turn or remove pages. Drag a page to its new place; on a touch screen, long-press it first. Or tap a page to choose it, then use the buttons above the grid to move it earlier or later, turn it left or right, or remove it.
  4. Add pages where you need them. Press Add pages, then Pages from a PDF or Blank page. The new pages go after the chosen page, or at the end when no page is chosen.
  5. Save the new PDF. Check the File name and press Save PDF. Then press Save to Downloads or Open; Share and Save as… also show where your browser offers them.

What you can do with Reorganize PDF

  • Every page shows in a grid, turned the way it will be saved. Tap a page to choose it; tap it again to let it go.
  • With a keyboard, Ctrl or ⌘ and the arrow keys move a page too.
  • Turn the chosen page left or right, a quarter turn at a time, or remove it. The page that takes its place is chosen next, so you can remove several in a row.
  • Pages from a PDF adds every page of another PDF after the chosen page, or at the end. On a computer, dropping or pasting a PDF on the screen does the same.
  • Blank page adds an empty page the same size as the page before it, lying sideways if you turned that page a quarter turn.
  • Undo takes back one change at a time, up to 50, and Reset pages brings back the PDF’s own pages.
  • The pages you keep still have their bookmarks, links, form fields and page labels (such as “iv”). An added PDF’s bookmarks and form fields come along with its pages.
  • The new PDF is named after yours, such as Report_reorganized.pdf, and you can change the File name before you save.

Your files stay on your device

Usugami works on your files right in your browser. Nothing is uploaded: the files you pick and the PDFs it makes never leave your device.

There are no ads, no tracking and no cookies, and you don’t need an account. Tools you have used once keep working offline while your browser keeps their files; a text-recognition language or font used for the first time needs the internet.

Questions

Can I add a blank page to a PDF?

Yes. Tap the page the blank page should follow, then press Add pages and Blank page. It takes that page’s size; with no page chosen, it goes at the end, the size of the last page.

Can I turn all the pages at once here?

Not in Reorganize PDF, which turns the chosen page a quarter turn at a time. To turn every page at once, use Rotate PDF and the buttons beside All pages.

Are removed pages really gone from the file?

Yes. Their text, the pictures only they used, their form fields and comments aren’t left hidden in the new PDF.

Can I reorganize a password-protected PDF?

Yes. Usugami asks for the password when you open it. The new PDF has no password, and the result screen tells you so.

Is Usugami free?

Yes. Usugami is free and open source, with no ads, no account and nothing to buy.

Does Usugami put its name on my PDFs?

Not on its pages, unless you turn on the Usugami watermark switch on the result screen, which adds a small “Usugami” in the corner of each page. Once on, it stays on for the PDFs you make next until you turn it off. The PDF’s document properties do name Usugami as the program that made it.

Which browsers does it work in?

Current Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, on phones, tablets and computers. Some buttons, such as Share and Save as…, show only in browsers that support them. On an Android phone you can also install the Usugami app.
