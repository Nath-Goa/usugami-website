Convert PNG to PDF

Images to PDF turns PNG images, such as screenshots and drawings, into a PDF in your browser. Transparent parts become white, and each page can be a paper size or the size of its image.

How to convert PNG to PDF

  1. Open the tool. Press the Open Images to PDF button at the top of this page. The tool opens in your browser, on a phone, tablet or computer, with nothing to install and nothing to sign up for.
  2. Add your PNG images. Press Photos or Files and pick one or more PNG images. You can also drop them onto the page, or paste an image you have copied.
  3. Put the pages in order. Drag a page to move it (on a touch screen, long-press it first). Or press Sort and pick Name (A to Z): numbers in names count as numbers, so Screenshot_2 comes before Screenshot_10.
  4. Choose the page size. For screenshots, set Page size to Fit (same as image), which makes each page the size of its image. To print, pick a paper size such as A4 and a Margin.
  5. Create the PDF. Press Convert to PDF, check the File name and Compression, then press Create PDF.
  6. Save your PDF. Press Save to Downloads. If you made a PDF for each image, Share or save as ZIP puts them all in one ZIP file.

What Images to PDF does with PNG images

  • Transparent parts of a PNG become white in the PDF, and half-transparent parts are blended with white, as if the picture were printed on paper.
  • A PNG left on the Original look that needs no shrinking for its page, such as a small screenshot or a diagram in plain colours, is stored without loss when the PNG file is no bigger than a JPEG copy would be. Other PNGs are stored as JPEG at the Compression level you pick.
  • Fit (same as image) makes each page the size of its image, so with No margin a screenshot fills its page.
  • A long screenshot keeps its text readable: on a Fit (same as image) page it is shrunk only when its shorter side has more pixels than an A4 page’s at the level’s resolution (about 1,240 at Recommended).
  • Paper sizes A4, US Letter, US Legal, A3 and A5 for printing, with No margin, a Small one or a Big one.
  • One PDF with a page for each image, or a separate PDF for each image, named after it.
  • Text notes and other hidden metadata saved in a PNG are left out of the PDF.
  • PNG images can share a PDF with JPG, HEIC, WebP, BMP and GIF images.

Your files stay on your device

Usugami works on your files right in your browser. Nothing is uploaded: the files you pick and the PDFs it makes never leave your device.

There are no ads, no tracking and no cookies, and you don’t need an account. Tools you have used once keep working offline while your browser keeps their files. A few parts download the first time they’re needed, such as a text-recognition language, a font or the HEIC photo decoder, and need the internet then.

Questions

What happens to a transparent background?

It becomes white. Usugami fills the see-through parts of a PNG with white, as paper would show through them in print.

How do I put several screenshots in one PDF?

Add them all, then press Sort and pick Name (A to Z) if their names are numbered. Press Convert to PDF: with Merge all images into one PDF on, Create PDF makes one PDF with a page for each screenshot.

Is a PNG converted without losing quality?

Sometimes. A PNG that isn’t shrunk for its page and is no bigger than a JPEG copy would be is stored without loss. Any other PNG is stored as JPEG, at 200, 150 or 100 dpi for the space it fills on its page; pick Less for the best quality.

Can I search the text of a screenshot in the PDF?

Yes. Turn on Make text searchable (OCR) in Create PDF and pick the Text language: English, French, Spanish or Hindi. Usugami reads the words in your images and adds them to the PDF as text you can search, select and copy.

Is Usugami free?

Yes. Usugami is free and open source, with no ads, no account and nothing to buy.

Does Usugami put its name on my PDFs?

Not on its pages, unless you turn on the Usugami watermark switch on the result screen, which adds a small “Usugami” in the corner of each page. Once on, it stays on for the PDFs you make next until you turn it off. The PDFs it makes do name Usugami in their document properties, as the program that made them.

Which browsers does it work in?

Current Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, on phones, tablets and computers. Some buttons, such as Share and Save as…, show only in browsers that support them. On an Android phone you can also install the Usugami app.
