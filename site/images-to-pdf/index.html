Create PDF

Convert images to PDF

Images to PDF turns JPG, PNG, HEIC, WebP, BMP and GIF images into a PDF in your browser. Put the pages in order, choose a page size and margin, and make one PDF or one for each image.

How to convert images to PDF

  1. Open the tool. Press the Open Images to PDF button at the top of this page. The tool opens in your browser, on a phone, tablet or computer, with nothing to install and nothing to sign up for.
  2. Add your images. Press Photos or Files and pick one or more images. You can also drop or paste images onto the page.
  3. Put the pages in order. Drag a page to move it (on a touch screen, long-press it first), or press Sort. Each page has buttons to turn it, remove it or crop it.
  4. Choose the page layout. Set Page size, Orientation and Margin. Each page shows as it will look in the PDF.
  5. Create the PDF. Press Convert to PDF, check the File name and Compression, then press Create PDF.
  6. Save your PDF. Press Save to Downloads. With several PDFs, Share or save as ZIP puts them in one ZIP file.

What you can do with Images to PDF

  • JPG, PNG, HEIC, WebP, BMP and GIF images, and AVIF in browsers that can read it. When the browser can’t read a HEIC photo, Usugami decodes it itself.
  • Page sizes Fit (same as image), A4, US Letter, US Legal, A3 and A5, with No margin, a Small one or a Big one. On a paper size, Orientation can be Auto, Portrait or Landscape, and Fit (same as image) follows each image’s shape. Your choices are remembered for next time.
  • Sort by Name (A to Z), Name (Z to A) or Reverse order. Names sort the way people expect, so IMG_2 comes before IMG_10.
  • Merge all images into one PDF makes one PDF with a page for each image. Turn it off and each image becomes a separate PDF, named after it.
  • Three Compression levels: Less (200 dpi), Recommended (150 dpi) and Extreme (100 dpi).
  • The crop button on each page opens Crop page and Filters, to cut the page out of a photo and give it a look such as Enhanced or Black & white.
  • Make text searchable (OCR) adds searchable text in English, French, Spanish or Hindi.
  • Photos come out the right way up, and their location and other hidden metadata are left out of the PDF.

Your files stay on your device

Usugami works on your files right in your browser. Nothing is uploaded: the files you pick and the PDFs it makes never leave your device.

There are no ads, no tracking and no cookies, and you don’t need an account. Tools you have used once keep working offline while your browser keeps their files. A few parts download the first time they’re needed, such as a text-recognition language, a font or the HEIC photo decoder, and need the internet then.

Questions

How do I convert HEIC photos to PDF?

Add them like any other image, with Photos or Files. Usugami reads them on your device, even in a browser that can’t open HEIC itself.

Can I make a separate PDF for each image?

Yes. With two or more images, turn off Merge all images into one PDF in Create PDF, and each image becomes its own PDF, named after it. Save to Downloads saves them all at once.

What page size does the PDF use?

A4, Portrait, with No margin, until you change it. Fit (same as image) makes each page the size of its image, and Auto orientation gives a wide image a landscape page.

How much are the images compressed?

With Less, Recommended or Extreme, each image is shrunk to 200, 150 or 100 dpi for the space it fills on its page, or with Fit (same as image), as if it filled an A4 page. Images are never enlarged, and a JPEG photo left on Original, with no brightness or contrast change, can go in as it is, without its metadata, when that is smaller than the shrunk copy.

Is Usugami free?

Yes. Usugami is free and open source, with no ads, no account and nothing to buy.

Does Usugami put its name on my PDFs?

Not on its pages, unless you turn on the Usugami watermark switch on the result screen, which adds a small “Usugami” in the corner of each page. Once on, it stays on for the PDFs you make next until you turn it off. The PDFs it makes do name Usugami in their document properties, as the program that made them.

Which browsers does it work in?

Current Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, on phones, tablets and computers. Some buttons, such as Share and Save as…, show only in browsers that support them. On an Android phone you can also install the Usugami app.
