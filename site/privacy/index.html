The Android app’s policy

Usugami Privacy Policy

Effective date: 28 November 2026. This is the privacy policy of the Android app, as published in PRIVACY.md in Usugami’s repository. It is also inside the app.

Usugami is an Android app that turns images, Word documents, PowerPoint presentations, Excel workbooks and EPUB e-books into PDFs; opens, annotates, prints, splits, merges, reorganizes, compresses, signs, redacts and translates PDFs; and turns PDFs into images or text. It works on your phone. It has no ads, no analytics of its own, no tracking, no accounts and no in-app purchases, and it does not collect, share or sell any data about you. In Usugami (the main edition), Google’s libraries inside the app hand records of their use to Google Play services on your phone, which may send them to Google (see below).

This policy covers all three editions of Usugami: “Usugami” (the main edition, Usugami.apk, from GitHub and Obtainium only), “Usugami Lite” and “Usugami OCR”. The app called “Usugami” in Usugami’s F-Droid repository is Usugami OCR, and so is the edition made for Google Play (Usugami-ocr.aab): they have no Translate PDF, no internet permission and no Google code. The differences that matter for privacy are text recognition (OCR) and, in the main edition only, Translate PDF and the internet permission its language packs need, explained below.

Who is responsible

Usugami is made by Nathan Kumtakar, an independent developer. It is free and open-source software: https://github.com/Nath-Goa/usugami

Questions or concerns: open an issue at https://github.com/Nath-Goa/usugami/issues. Issues are public, so please don’t include personal details. To report a security problem privately, use “Report a vulnerability” on the repository’s Security tab.

What Usugami can access

  • Only the images, PDFs, Word, PowerPoint and Excel files, and EPUB books you choose. You pick them with Android’s photo picker or file picker, you share them to Usugami from another app, or you open them with Usugami from another app (“Open with”). Android then gives Usugami access to those files only, and to a folder you pick to save into (see Recent files below). Usugami cannot browse your photos or any other files or folders.
  • Usugami asks for no permissions: no storage, camera, location, contacts or microphone access. Usugami (the main edition) declares two permissions that Android grants when it installs an app, without asking: internet access, and seeing whether the phone is online. They are there only for Translate PDF’s language packs (see below). Usugami Lite and Usugami OCR declare neither.
  • Take photo opens your phone’s own camera app, which saves just that one photo for Usugami. Usugami never uses the camera itself, and the photo stays in Usugami’s private working copies until you make it into a PDF or remove it.
  • Usugami Lite and Usugami OCR have no internet permission, so Android does not let them connect to the internet, and they cannot upload your files. Usugami (the main edition) has it only so that Android’s download manager can download Translate PDF’s language packs. Usugami’s own code opens no connection, and it does not upload your files (see “Translate PDF” below).
  • When you save a file, it goes where you choose: the Downloads folder or a folder you pick with the system file picker. When you tap Share or Open with another app, the file goes to the app you pick, and that app’s own privacy policy applies. When you tap Print, the PDF goes to Android’s print screen and then to the printer or print service you choose there.

What Usugami stores on your phone

  • Working copies: while you work, Usugami keeps copies of the files you picked or opened and the files it makes in its private cache on your phone, where other apps can’t read them. Usugami deletes them automatically once they are no longer needed (for example, when you start over), and Android may also clear the cache when storage runs low.
  • Signatures: a signature you draw for “Sign PDF” is kept only while Usugami is open, in its memory and in the screen state Android keeps for it, so you can use it again. It is never saved in a file of its own, only in the PDFs you sign.
  • Annotate: the text, drawings and shapes you add to a PDF in the reader are kept the same way, until you close it, and only end up in the PDF you save. Pictures you add are working copies, as above. The file you opened is never changed.
  • Settings: your last choices (such as page size, margins and compression level), stored privately on your phone.
  • Recent files: the name, type, size and place of the last 50 files you saved with Usugami, and when you saved them, stored privately on your phone so you can open and share them again from Home. Usugami can read the files it saved to Downloads without any permission. For a file saved with “Save as”, Usugami keeps Android’s permission to read that file while it is on the list. For files saved into a folder you picked, it keeps Android’s permission to read that folder, and what is in it, while one of them is on the list, and uses it only to open, share and check on those files. Usugami gives each permission back once no file on the list needs it.
  • Language packs (Usugami, the main edition): the packs Translate PDF has downloaded, about 45 MB each once unpacked, in Usugami’s private storage. Beside them, Google’s libraries keep a random identifier ML Kit makes for this installation, the packs’ checksums and a database of usage events. Google’s ML Kit data disclosure lists identifiers of this kind among what ML Kit collects with its usage records (see “Translate PDF” below). They keep no Firebase identifier, token or settings.
  • Usugami excludes its data from Android’s cloud backup and device-to-device transfer.

Usugami stores nothing anywhere else, and it has no servers. Outside Usugami’s own storage, two things on your phone hold traces of its use in Usugami (the main edition): the phone’s system log holds, for a while, the text Translate PDF translated and the translation (see “Translate PDF” below), and Google Play services keeps the text-recognition models and receives the usage records Google’s libraries hand it (see “Text recognition” and “Translate PDF” below).

Text recognition (OCR)

Text recognition is optional. It only runs when you turn on “Make text searchable (OCR)” while converting images, or “Read pages without text” in PDF to images or text, or, in the main edition, when Translate PDF comes to a scanned page. The recognition itself happens on your phone. Your images and the recognized text are never sent to Google or anyone else. How it works depends on the edition.

Usugami (the main edition)

  • Text recognition uses Google ML Kit, a library made by Google, and runs through Google Play services, a separate app from Google that is installed on most Android phones.
  • The first time you use a text language, Google Play services downloads Google’s text-recognition model for it (one model covers English, French, Spanish, German, Portuguese and Indonesian; Hindi has its own). This needs an internet connection at that time. Google Play services then keeps the model for later use; if it removes a model, it downloads it again the next time you use that language. Google Play services does these downloads, not Usugami, and Google’s Privacy Policy applies to them: https://policies.google.com/privacy
  • To download and run text recognition, Google’s libraries inside Usugami talk to Google Play services on your phone. Google Play services may report to Google how these Google APIs were used (for example, whether a call succeeded).
  • According to Google’s ML Kit data disclosure, ML Kit may also collect diagnostic and usage information about the ML Kit feature, such as device and app information and performance metrics, and send it to Google: https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/android-data-disclosure
  • Your images and the recognized text are not part of any of this.
  • Usugami disables (unregisters) the part of Google’s library that would upload ML Kit’s metrics from inside Usugami, so those metrics are dropped. Google Play services is a separate app, and what it sends is covered by Google’s policies, not by Usugami.
  • Everything else in Usugami (the reader, images, Word, PowerPoint, Excel and EPUB to PDF, and all the PDF tools but Translate PDF) works without Google Play services and without internet.

Usugami OCR

  • Text recognition is open source and built into the app, models included: PaddleOCR’s PP-OCRv5 models, run on your phone’s processor by Tencent’s ncnn. It works with no internet connection and doesn’t need Google Play services.
  • The page images are read in the app’s memory and only the recognized text is kept, as the PDF’s invisible text layer or in the text file PDF to images or text makes. Nothing is sent anywhere or stored anywhere else.
  • Usugami OCR contains no Google libraries and makes no network connections at all.

Usugami Lite

  • Usugami Lite has no text recognition. It contains no Google libraries and makes no network connections at all.

Translate PDF (Usugami, the main edition, only)

Translate PDF makes a copy of a PDF in another language. It is only in Usugami, the main edition; Usugami Lite and Usugami OCR don’t have it, its permission or its Google code.

  • On your phone. The translating is done on your phone, by Google ML Kit’s translation library inside Usugami. The PDF, its text and the translation are not sent to Google or anyone else. The PDF’s language is also worked out on the phone, by a library whose model is built into the app.
  • Language packs. Translating from or into a language needs that language’s pack; English is built in. The first time a translation needs a pack, Usugami asks you first. If you agree, Android’s download manager, a part of Android outside Usugami, downloads it from Google’s servers: about 40 MB, once per language. As with any download, Google’s servers receive the request, which comes from your phone’s internet address and names the language pack being downloaded, and Google’s Privacy Policy applies to it: https://policies.google.com/privacy. Android counts the download as Usugami’s in its data usage. If you cancel, the download stops and a pack that hadn’t finished is removed. A pack then stays on your phone, and translating needs no internet, until you delete it under Language packs on Translate PDF’s screen.
  • Why the internet permission. Android’s download manager only downloads for an app that has the internet permission, so Usugami has it. Seeing whether the phone is online lets Usugami tell you, before and during a download, when there is no connection.
  • Usugami itself opens no connection. From the moment Usugami starts, its own process refuses to open any web address, to any server. Google’s translation library would otherwise register itself with Google’s Firebase service and fetch settings from it; those attempts are refused inside the app before any connection is made, and the network library the registration would use is left out of Usugami. A recording of the network traffic of Usugami’s release build, made on an emulator, showed traffic only while a pack downloaded, from Google’s download servers, and none when translating again, hours or days later, offline or left idle.
  • Usage records. As with text recognition, using the translation library hands a record of its use to Google Play services on your phone, which may send it to Google. According to Google’s ML Kit data disclosure (above), such records can hold, among other things, the phone’s maker, model and Android version, the app’s name and version, identifiers for the device and for this installation, how much text went in and out and how long it took, and, for Translate PDF, the languages chosen and the language detected. What Google Play services sends is covered by Google’s policies, not by Usugami.
  • The system log. Google’s translation library writes the text it translates, and the translation, into the phone’s system log. Other apps you install can’t read the system log, but a computer connected to the phone for USB debugging can, and so can a bug report made on the phone, which you might send to someone. Android keeps the log in memory, where newer lines replace older ones, and on most phones a restart clears it. Usugami keeps the Java code of Google’s libraries from writing debug and info messages (their warnings and errors, which hold no text, stay), but not these lines, which come from the library’s native code: silencing them would take native code of Usugami’s own, and only Android 11 and newer allow it.
  • Scanned pages are read with text recognition first, as described above for the main edition, including its own first download through Google Play services.

If you want no network use of any kind, install Usugami OCR or Usugami Lite.

Data collection and sharing

Usugami does not collect personal data. It has no analytics of its own, crash reporting, advertising IDs or accounts; in Usugami (the main edition), what Google’s libraries hand to Google Play services is described above. The developer receives nothing from the app and cannot see what you do with it.

Children

Usugami does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • Working copies are deleted automatically (see above).
  • To take a file off the Recent files list, or clear the list, use the Recent files screen (See all on Home). The files themselves stay where you saved them.
  • To delete everything Usugami stores on your phone, go to Settings > Apps > Usugami > Storage and tap “Clear storage”, or uninstall Usugami.
  • In Usugami (the main edition), clearing its storage doesn’t remove the lines Translate PDF’s library wrote to the phone’s system log; on most phones, restarting the phone does.
  • PDFs you saved to Downloads or another folder are your files. They stay until you delete them, even if you uninstall Usugami.
  • In Usugami (the main edition), the text-recognition models are kept and managed by Google Play services. In Usugami OCR they are part of the app and go when you uninstall it.
  • In Usugami (the main edition), Translate PDF’s language packs stay until you delete them under Language packs on its screen, clear Usugami’s storage or uninstall it. Installing Usugami OCR over Usugami keeps them, and Usugami OCR can’t show or delete them: delete them before you switch, or clear its storage.

Changes to this policy

Any change to this policy is made in the file PRIVACY.md in Usugami’s public repository, so every version and change is visible in its history: https://github.com/Nath-Goa/usugami/commits/main/PRIVACY.md

The effective date at the top changes with each update, and app releases include the version of the policy that applies to them.

This website and the web app

Usugami on the web

Effective date: 9 November 2026.

This part covers this website and the web app on it (Usugami in your browser). They have no ads, no analytics, no tracking and no accounts, and, like the Android app, they do not collect, share or sell any data about you.

This website

  • No cookies, analytics, ads or tracking, and nothing is loaded from other sites: no outside fonts, scripts or images.
  • The website is a set of static files hosted by GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub receives your IP address and the details of each request (such as the page asked for and your browser) when you visit. GitHub’s privacy statement applies to that: https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement
  • Links to other sites, such as GitHub, Obtainium, F-Droid or Buy Me a Coffee (the “Buy me a coffee” button), take you to those sites, and their own privacy policies apply there.

The web app

  • The web app works on your files in your browser, on your device. It never uploads them: the images, PDFs and documents you pick, and the PDFs it makes, never leave your device.
  • It can read only the files you pick, drop on it, paste into it, share to it or open with it. It doesn’t use your camera, location, contacts or microphone: Take photo opens your phone’s own camera app, which hands just that one photo to the web app. It saves only where you choose; with Save to folder…, your browser asks you first to let it save into the folder you picked.
  • It downloads only its own files, from this same website, and only as they are needed: the app itself, and the libraries a tool uses the first time you open that tool. These requests ask for the app’s files by name and carry nothing of your files.
  • When you save a file, your browser saves it where you choose. When you open a file, your browser shows it in a new tab (a ZIP file is saved instead). When you share a file, it goes to the app you pick, and that app’s own privacy policy applies.

Text recognition (OCR) and fonts

  • Text recognition is optional. It only runs when you turn on “Make text searchable (OCR)” while converting images, and the recognition itself happens in your browser, on your device.
  • The first time you use a text language, the web app downloads the text-recognition engine (Tesseract) and the data for that language from this same website: about 5 to 8 MB, depending on the language. This needs an internet connection at that time. Your browser then keeps them in its cache for later use; if it removes them, they are downloaded again the next time you use that language.
  • Your images and the recognized text never leave your device. They are not sent to this website, to Google or to anyone else. Only the recognized text is kept, as the PDF’s invisible text layer.
  • Fonts work the same way: the first time a tool needs a font to write text into a PDF (for example Word to PDF, Add page numbers, Add watermark, Edit PDF or text recognition), the web app downloads it from this same website, and your browser keeps it in its cache. Your text is not sent anywhere.

What the web app stores on your device

  • Working copies: while you work, the files you picked and the PDFs the web app makes are kept only in the page’s memory, not in your browser’s storage. They are gone when you start over, leave the tool or close the page.
  • Signatures: a signature you draw for “Sign PDF” is kept only in the page’s memory while the web app is open, so you can use it again. It is never saved in your browser’s storage or in a file of its own, only in the PDFs you sign.
  • Edit PDF: the text, drawings, shapes and pictures you add to a PDF are kept the same way, until you leave the tool, and only end up in the PDF you save.
  • Settings: your language, your appearance (System, Light or Dark), your last choices in the tools (such as page size, orientation, margins, one PDF or one per image, and compression level), and the Usugami watermark switch with its size and opacity. They are kept in your browser’s local storage on your device. This website’s pages read the appearance setting to match the app’s theme, and never write anything.
  • Recent files: copies of the last 50 files you saved with the web app, with their names, types and sizes and when you saved them, kept in your browser’s storage (IndexedDB) on your device so you can open and share them again from Home. A browser can’t open a file it has saved to your device again, so the web app keeps its own copy, in this browser only. Removing a file from the list, or clearing the list, deletes its copy. Files you only share aren’t listed.
  • Offline copies: the web app’s service worker keeps copies of the site’s own files (the app, its libraries, fonts and text-recognition data, as they are used) in your browser’s cache, so the web app, and each tool you have used, works without an internet connection. They are the same files for everyone and contain nothing of yours.
  • Shared files: when you share files to the installed web app from another app, your browser hands them to its service worker, which keeps them in your browser’s cache only until the web app has read them, and then deletes them. If the web app is closed before it reads them, they are deleted the next time you share files to it (once they are an hour old), or when you clear this site’s data. Sharing works only through the service worker: after you clear this site’s data, open the web app once before you share files to it again. Without its service worker, your browser would send the shared files to this website instead.
  • Keeping it: once you install the web app, it asks your browser to keep what it stores here (persistent storage) instead of clearing it by itself when your device runs short of space. Your browser may ask you first. It changes nothing about what is stored, and clearing this site’s data still deletes it all.

Nothing is stored anywhere else, and nothing is sent to the developer or anyone else. The web app has no servers of its own: this website only serves its files.

Deleting it

  • Working copies and Edit PDF’s additions go by themselves when you leave the tool or close the page, and a signature when you close or reload the page.
  • To take a file off Recent files, or empty it, use the Recent files screen (See all on Home) and Remove from list or Clear list. The files you saved stay where you saved them.
  • To delete everything the web app stores (settings, Recent files and offline copies), clear this site’s data in your browser’s settings. Files you saved to your device stay until you delete them.

Data collection

The web app and this website do not collect personal information from anyone, including children. The developer receives nothing and cannot see what you do with them.
